## Identification

The pedunculate oak is a large, long-lived deciduous tree with a broad, spreading crown.

The most useful identification features are:

* deeply lobed leaves with very short leaf stalks;
* acorns carried on conspicuously long stalks;
* rounded leaf lobes;
* grey-brown bark that becomes deeply fissured with age;
* a broad crown on mature trees.

The long stalks of the acorns are the origin of the common name **pedunculate oak**.

## Leaves

The leaves are typically dark green above and paler beneath. They have several rounded lobes and a characteristic very short stalk.

Leaves are generally 10–20 cm long, although size varies with age, position and growing conditions.

## Bark

Young branches have relatively smooth grey-brown bark. As the tree matures, the bark becomes thicker and develops deep vertical fissures.

The bark provides habitat for numerous organisms, including lichens, mosses, fungi and invertebrates.

## Flowers and acorns

Pedunculate oak flowers in spring.

Male flowers occur in hanging catkins, while female flowers are inconspicuous. Following pollination, the familiar acorns develop during summer and mature in autumn.

The acorns are attached to long stalks, providing one of the easiest ways to distinguish <em>Quercus robur</em> from some closely related European oaks.

## Habitat and distribution

<em>Quercus robur</em> is native to much of Europe and occurs in a wide range of habitats.

It is particularly characteristic of:

* deciduous and mixed woodland;
* river valleys and floodplains;
* parkland and pasture;
* hedgerows;
* urban and historic landscapes.

The species tolerates a range of soils and environmental conditions, although it generally performs best where sufficient moisture is available.

## Seasonal cycle

**Spring**
New leaves emerge and the tree flowers.

**Summer**
The crown is fully developed and young acorns begin to develop.

**Autumn**
Acorns mature and fall. Leaves turn yellow-brown and are shed.

**Winter**
The tree is leafless, making the branching structure, bark and buds particularly visible.

## Ecological importance

Pedunculate oak is an important ecological species in European landscapes.

Mature and veteran oaks can support exceptionally diverse communities of:

* insects and other invertebrates;
* fungi and lichens;
* mosses;
* birds;
* bats;
* small mammals.

Acorns are an important food resource for wildlife, while cavities, dead wood and fissured bark provide shelter and nesting opportunities.
